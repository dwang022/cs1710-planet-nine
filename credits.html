Planet Nine has never been seen. Everything on this site about it is inferred from the orbits of other objects, not observed.

Data

Downloaded in October 2026. Every number on the site is either computed from these files when the page loads or tagged with where it came from.

  • JPL Small-Body Database The orbit of every known object whose average distance from the Sun is more than 30 times Earth’s. ssd.jpl.nasa.gov
  • Minor Planet Center A second orbit catalogue to check JPL’s against, the discovery records of numbered objects, every reported position of the 90 extreme objects and the codes that place each observatory on the map. minorplanetcenter.net
  • JPL planetary elements and Horizons The planets’ orbits. Horizons also checked our orbit calculations and gave Voyager 1’s position. ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets
  • Natural Earth The coastlines and borders on the observatory map, through the world-atlas package. Public domain. naturalearthdata.com
  • Crossref and arXiv The titles, dates and abstracts of the papers. Every claim about a paper was checked against its abstract. papers/README.md

The full list of sources, with the exact queries, is in data/SOURCES.md.

Photographs and logos

The photographs come from Wikimedia Commons, with each credit and licence taken from Commons itself. The one exception is Keystone College’s telescope, used under fair use for this class project. The game’s photos are public data from the NSF NOIRLab Astro Data Archive and the Canadian Astronomy Data Centre, credited as each archive asks. A logo only identifies its organisation and implies no endorsement.

Drawings and code

  • Drawings The planets, the observatories and every version of Planet Nine are our own drawings, made in SVG. The look of the planet chart is borrowed from the one in the game Outer Wilds.
  • Code The charts are made with d3 and the site is built with Vite. github.com/dwang022
