One caution

Planet Nine has never been seen. Everything on this site describes an inference from the orbits of other objects, not an observation.

Data

Fetched in October 2026. Every number on the site is computed from these files on load or carries a tag naming its source.

  • JPL Small-Body Database
    Orbits of every known body more than 30 times farther from the Sun than Earth, from the Query API.     ssd.jpl.nasa.gov
  • Minor Planet Center
    The independent orbit catalogue used to cross-check JPL, the discovery records of numbered minor planets, every reported position of the 90 extreme objects, and the list of observatory codes that places each telescope.     minorplanetcenter.net
  • JPL planetary elements and Horizons
    The planets' own orbits; Horizons verified our geometry and gave Voyager 1's position.     ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets
  • Natural Earth
    The coastlines of the telescope map, through the world-atlas package. Public domain.     naturalearthdata.com
  • Crossref and arXiv
    Titles, dates and abstracts of the papers; every claim about a paper was checked against its abstract.     papers/README.md

The full list of sources, with the exact queries, is in data/SOURCES.md.

    Photographs and logos

    The photographs are from Wikimedia Commons, their credit and licence read from the Commons API, not typed, except Keystone College's telescope, used under fair use for this class project. The game's exposures are public data from the NSF NOIRLab Astro Data Archive and the Canadian Astronomy Data Centre, credited as each archive asks. A logo identifies its organisation and implies no endorsement.

      Drawings and code

      The planets, the telescope and every Planet Nine are our own drawings, written as SVG. The charts are built with d3 and the site with Vite. The planetary chart takes its look from the one in Outer Wilds.
