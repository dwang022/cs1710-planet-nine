    How the cartoon works
    • The make-believe orbits have shapes like the real ones and start in random directions, the same in both pictures. Without a planet each drifts a little. With one, an orbit pointing within —° of the planet's direction is flung away when the planet next passes it; the rest ease round to the side opposite it.
    • In full simulations of the planet's gravity, an object kicked by a close pass is sent onto a wild orbit: thrown out of the solar system, or in among the giant planets, which scatter it further. The survivors are protected by resonances: they go round a whole number of times for each of the planet's orbits, which keeps them clear of it.
    • The real dynamics also tilt the orbits, take hundreds of millions of years to settle, and depend on the planet's mass and orbit, none of which this cartoon tries to capture.
