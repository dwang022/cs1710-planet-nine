    How the cartoon works
    • The orbits start pointing every which way. With the planet, those within —° of its orbit's direction are flung when it passes; the rest settle opposite it.
    • A planet only clears out what it meets, not every orbit that crosses its path. An orbit pointing the same way runs alongside the planet's, so the two meet sooner or later. One pointing the opposite way crosses it at just two points, and the planet's pull keeps the object in a rhythm that never brings it there when the planet is.
    • A flung object is thrown out of the solar system or in among the giant planets.
    • Left out: real orbits are tilted, so crossings on this flat picture often miss in space (for every orbit, flung or not); they take hundreds of millions of years to settle; and they depend on the planet's mass and orbit.
