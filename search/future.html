How Rubin searches

  1. Every few nights, Rubin photographs the southern sky.

  2. Software flags anything that moved since an earlier photo.

  3. The smaller a slow dot’s yearly loop, the farther away it is.

  4. That far out, the dot’s brightness shows whether it is planet-sized.

Rubin should be able to see all but the faintest and most northern of Planet Nine’s predicted positions2.

Other searches

  • ProposedChecking the infrared candidates

    Two old infrared surveys caught dots that might be the planet’s faint warmth3. New deep photos could check them.

  • ProposedListening for its heat

    Even a cold planet glows faintly in microwaves, a glow more sensitive telescopes could detect4.

  • Under wayTesting the clustering

    Rubin’s new distant orbits will show whether the clustering holds up.
