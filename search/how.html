From Earth to the sky

One patch of sky at a time

  • From any spot on Earth, half the sky is below the horizon, and a telescope photographs only a small patch of the other half at a time.
  • Astronomers picture the sky as a sphere around Earth: every direction is a point on it.
From Earth to the sky

Unrolling the sphere

  • To show every direction at once, the sphere is unrolled into an oval, the way a world map unrolls the globe.
  • Across the oval is position around the sky. Up and down is how far north or south of the celestial equator, the sky’s own equator, straight above Earth’s.

Astronomers call the two right ascension and declination.
