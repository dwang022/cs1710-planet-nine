4 / 9

Because a photo can be too shallow

Planet Nine would be extremely faint: magnitude 22 to 25. On this scale bigger means fainter, and your eye gives out at about 6.

Each area is now shaded by how faint its photos can see. Brighter means deeper. Only a few small patches are bright. Most of the sky has only been photographed to about magnitude 22.

Only these slivers are deep enough

Just two surveys reach magnitude 23.5, the depth needed to catch a faint Planet Nine. Together they cover about —% of the sky.

And part of it is lost in the Milky Way

The pale band is our own galaxy, seen edge-on. Where the path crosses it, there are so many stars that picking out one faint, slow-moving dot is nearly hopeless, even in a deep photo.

The search that could settle it

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory (pink outline) is surveying the southern sky to magnitude 24.5 from 2026 to 2036. That is deep enough.

It covers —% of the unsearched stretches of the path. It should either find Planet Nine or leave very few places for it to hide.
