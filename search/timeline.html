Ten years of argument

The major papers, one line each. Green rows argue for a planet, orange rows against one, and violet rows are the data and searches, with their finders' conclusions hanging beneath. Larger rows are the papers the field answers to.

Which lane a paper lands in is our reading of its abstract, not a label the authors chose. Every paper we read is listed in papers/README.md.

How the proposal changed

Over the decade the proposed planet has moved closer and grown lighter, as searches ruled out the bright, nearby possibilities and new objects tightened the fit. The orange bar is a candidate, not a fit.

Values and ranges are read from each paper's abstract or figures, as tagged in the data; the 2016 founding paper gave only a mass floor and a notional orbit. Two readings of Brown & Batygin (2021) are shown: the headline numbers in its abstract and the full fit from its Figure 8, which is what this site draws everywhere else.

So far

A decade on, the argument is narrower but not over. The proposed planet is smaller, closer and fainter than it started; the strongest criticism has shifted from "the pattern is not there" to "the surveys made it". The last page looks at what could end the argument, and when.
